## What is Descriptive Analytics?
Descriptive Analytics is looking at your historical business data to understand _what happened ?_ Of course, what happened depends on your specific business and your specific use case. You might be interested in knowing how people shopped in your online store or how people serviced their debt for loans issued from your bank. This kind of analysis forms the basis or foundation for other types of analysis like _predictive_, or, _prescriptive_ analytics. A typical workflow is [discussed here](https://github.com/rajivsam/descriptive_analytics/blob/main/concept_discussion/operational_data_prep.md). Understanding what has happened is critical in designing better solutions for the future and avoiding mistakes from the past (those who ignore history are doomed to repeat it!), but sadly, this is one of the most undervalued areas of business analytics. Graphs are a very powerful analytical tool in descriptive analytics. A lot of data in the real world is relational in nature. Converting to graphs and analyzing them can yield very interesting insights. This repository focuses on descriptive analytics using graph-based methods. See the [relation to graph mapping document](https://github.com/rajivsam/descriptive_analytics/blob/main/examples/graph_from_relations/rel-to-graph-concepts.pdf) for details on how you can convert relational data to graphs for analysis.
## What kind of use cases does this repository cover?
This repository covers operational or analytical datasets that are relational. In particular, _Large Language Models_ are not part of any solution here. Solutions developed here utilize machine learning methods that have served the _knowledge discovery in databases_ for a long time.
